**Sources.** Four research tracks, all read-only and all reported as aggregates, with no personal
data:
1. Sales and traffic: the Shopify store (Apr 2025 – Sep 2026), Zoho CRM invoices (May–Sep 2026)

2. Voice of the customer: about 150 relevant email threads (Mar 2025 – Sep 2026), Klaviyo, the

## 1. First principles

- **The job.** An angler wants to see fish and his lure under the water, live, so he finds and
  catches fish faster. Every live (forward-facing) sonar does this job.
- **How the incumbents do it.** A transducer plus a dedicated display plus installation. Real
  spend is about $2,500 for a complete setup, about $4,000 for an ice package and about $5,500
  dealer-installed on a boat. The display is the expensive, heavy part.
- **Accuphy's core truth.** The high-resolution screen and processor are already in the angler's
  pocket or tackle bag. So live imaging starts at US$699 (PingLive Mini) or CA$1,749 (PingLive).
  It is the only phone/tablet live-imaging system on sale in North America that could be
  verified.
- **The same truth is the main weakness.** PingLive is a four-part system: transducer, router,
  the customer's battery and the customer's screen. Most confusion and many apparent faults
  happen where those parts meet:
  - which tablet, brightness in sun, battery in the cold;
  - fuses and wiring;
  - Wi-Fi versus the "5G" wording, which reads as cell coverage;
  - mounting.

  Customers can't tell where Accuphy's responsibility ends.
- **Trust decides more than specs.** For a new, China-developed brand, the purchase turns on
  independent proof, local availability and fast warranty service.
- **Anglers believe what they see working.** Friends (58%) and family (49%) are the top
  information sources. Fellow anglers are the most trusted (71%), then guides (40%). Under-45s
  also choose gear on YouTube. Live sonar is sold by seeing it on a friend's boat, at a dealer or
  in a video.

## 2. Who actually buys, and who we said we target

**What our own documents say:** the iCAST 2026 persona is men aged 30–45, competitive bass
anglers who like technology.

**What the evidence says:**
- **Online buyers are Canadian and seasonal.**
  - 77% of Shopify orders come from Canada: Ontario 159, Quebec 56, Manitoba 52, Alberta 40, BC
    23, Saskatchewan 22. The Prairies are 32%.
  - PingLive is 76% of online net sales.
  - **43% of orders fall in November–February** (the ice season and the holidays), and December
    is the peak.
  - The purchase is considered and one-time: 5% of buyers come back. 45% of orders used a
    discount.
- **The US is large but blocked.**
  - The US store sells the Mini only.
  - The US is 21% of orders but 39% of sessions, and it converts at about a third of Canada's
    rate. About 95% of US checkouts are abandoned.
  - 681 US subscribers have never ordered.
- **By volume, Accuphy is a B2B business.** From May to September 2026 about 94% of sonar units
  went through distributors and dealers. Europe is 81% of B2B value, and the Mini is 57% of B2B
  units.
- **Who writes to us:**
  - anglers who know LiveScope and want a lighter or second system (~25 threads);
  - first-time buyers priced out of the big brands (~12);
  - ice anglers (~15);
  - kayak and float-tube anglers (~10);
  - Quebec francophones (~10);
  - Chinese-speaking anglers in Canada who found us through Douyin, Xiaohongshu or WeChat (~7).
- **The market view:** tournament bass pros are the weakest volume target. They are locked into
  a brand, want screens of 10" or more, and live sonar is being restricted in their tournaments.

**Conclusion:** the real buyer is northern, practical, portable-minded and value-driven, not a
tournament bass pro.

## 4. What customers say

- **Before buying (ranked):**
  1. Can I buy it here, now? (US PingLive, stock-outs, EU checkout)
  2. Show me proof: independent reviews, demo units, real footage of a lure and fish.
  3. Total cost: freight, duties, a bright enough screen.
  4. Which screen, and does it work in sun, cold and heat?
  5. Will it fit my setup: poles, cell service, TV?
  6. Performance: depth claims, seeing a jig at 40–60 ft.
- **After buying (ranked):**
  1. Hardware faults from one production batch.
  2. **Slow support and warranty.** This hurts loyalty more than the faults themselves.
  3. Power and wiring.
  4. Screen and app.
  5. **Can't read the picture.** Coaching turns these owners into fans; Accuphy says setup takes
     3–5 trips.
  6. Shipping delays and mounting parts.

  Returns are 12.4% for the Mini against 6.6% for PingLive, which suggests an expectation gap on
  the entry model.
- **What delights them:**
  - Their own screen replaces the head unit and the black box ("genius", "LiveScope for Android
    and Apple").
  - Mobility.
  - Light and quick to set up.
  - Seeing the lure.
  - Combo View.
  - Free updates.
  - Fast replacements create fans.
- **Their words:** "live scope" as a generic name for any live sonar, FFS, forward sonar, head
  unit, brain box, graph, black box, "dial it in", "see my jig".
- **Their comparisons:** the consensus is that Garmin's picture is a little sharper. PingLive
  wins on price, weight, having no black box and portability. The Mini is judged against the
  Lowrance Eagle Eye 9.

## 5. Market

- **Who sells live sonar:**
  - Garmin, Lowrance and Humminbird, all needing their own display.
  - The Lowrance Eagle Eye 9, an all-in-one at $999 and the entry benchmark.
  - Garmin's LiveScope 2 (July 2026) drops the black box, which weakens our "simpler" claim.
- **Adoption is rising:**
  - about 30% of Minnesota anglers;
  - 40–72% on some Minnesota lakes;
  - over a third of crappie boats in an Arkansas study.

  But overall participation is softening: the US has 57M anglers, the first dip since 2021, and
  the population is aging.
- **Headwinds and growth:**
  - B.A.S.S. and MLF limit live sonar in tournaments, and the NPFL banned it.
  - Minnesota and Wisconsin are debating restrictions.
  - Ice and kayak fishing are growing, and 3 in 4 anglers fish in groups.
- **Accuphy's position:**
  - Strengths: cheapest route to live imaging, no display or subscription needed, light, several
    screens at once.
  - Weaknesses: unknown brand, no ecosystem, phone screens smaller than the 10" norm, cold and
    glare doubts, thin US dealer reach, very few reviews.

## 6. Our funnel today

- **Devices:** 72% of sessions are on mobile (iOS 46%). In Canada, mobile converts at 0.50%
  against 1.03% on desktop; the gap is at checkout completion, not at add-to-cart.
- **Sources:** direct 47% and Google 38% of sessions. Social is about 10% of sessions but only
  about 2% of last-click orders. **There is no campaign (UTM) tracking at all.**
- **Top landing pages** include the Android and Apple compatibility posts and the setup guide.
  Buyers check "will it work with my phone?".
- **Email:**
  - Waitlist offers and Black Friday perform best.
  - The abandoned-cart flow converts 3.1%.
  - About 1,700 subscribers (1,045 in Canada, 681 in the US) have never ordered.

## 7. Learnings for Accuphy Lab (proposals, waiting on Jason)

1. **The Lab replaces the friend's boat.** Its job is the "see it with your own eyes" moment that
   anglers otherwise get on a friend's boat or at a dealer. An honest picture beats hype:
   customers agree Garmin is a little sharper, so never claim parity.
2. **The strongest "aha" for our real buyers:** live sonar on the phone or tablet you already
   own. It combines the category wow, our unique truth and the price. Frame the price as "$699 +
   the tablet you have" against $2,500+ systems. The pending "aha" question (A / B / C in the
   mobile plan) points to A; B ("as clear as the big brands") contradicts what customers say.
3. **Scene.** The hero boat, a Ranger Z521R tournament bass boat, signals the persona the data
   says is not ours. Consider a 16–18 ft aluminium boat, a kayak and an ice-hole scene. Ice
   season is the buying peak, and the hole-hopper is P1.
4. **Timing and geography.** Any Lab advertising should target Canada before the ice season. The
   US should wait until its availability and checkout problems are fixed; otherwise the ad money
   is wasted.
5. **Mobile.** 72% of visits are mobile and mobile checkout is weak. That confirms the email
   sign-up for an offer as the Lab's main action (#45), with the email flows (which already
   work) turning leads into purchases.
6. **The Lab's second job is onboarding.** The labelled sonar picture can teach new owners and
   dealer staff to read the screen: fish, structure, bottom, their own lure. That targets the
   "can't read the picture" problem, the Mini's 12% returns and the support load.
7. **Show multi-device sharing.** Families, co-anglers and a TV in the ice shack are an angle
   nobody else claims, and 3 in 4 anglers fish in groups.
8. **Dealers and distributors carry most of the volume.** The Lab can be their demo, in store or
   on their sites. Add languages later; French first, since the French pages convert best.
9. **Measure before spending.** UTM tags, the Meta Pixel and Klaviyo source tags come before any
   ad budget.

## 8. Gaps: how to learn more

- **The HelpScout queue (~4,200 conversations since March 2025) was not analysed.** Connecting
  HelpScout and running a proper study would sharpen everything above.
- **Not yet checked:** RMA portal data (failure modes), Google Play reviews and the Accuphy
  Facebook group.
- **No owner survey exists.** Five questions would fill the biggest hole:
  1. How do you fish (ice, boat, kayak)?
  2. Which screen do you use?
  3. Why did you buy?
  4. What almost stopped you?
  5. Where did you hear about us?
- **No ad data exists,** and the April 2026 sales spike is still unexplained.
