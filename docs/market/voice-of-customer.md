## 2. Why people buy live sonar

1. See fish and how they react, live, and target the big ones (Lake Winnipeg greenbacks).
   [P] An ice angler saw up to 20 fish on live sonar that his flasher missed.
2. LiveScope-level vision for much less, **without replacing the fish finder they own**.
3. Portability: no black box, two wires, no cable to the screen (ice, kayak, rental boat, dock).
4. Keeping up with others, especially in kayak tournaments ([T] "Kayaks and Livescope - Done
   Deal"; [P] nearly every angler in one kayak series runs FFS).
5. Fun: watching fish come in from a heated shelter, fishing with kids and friends.
6. [Q] "our average size/weight went up considerably" (bass forum).

**Triggers:** ICAST reveal videos, the start of ice season (the Nodak thread opened 19 Dec),
fishing next to someone who has one, a cheap new brand appearing, a new boat or kayak,
tournament pressure.

## 4. How they choose

- **LiveScope is the benchmark** and the category's everyday name ("scope"; in French
  "livescope" is a common noun).
- They want to **try before buying**: rent one, fish beside an owner, see a demo.
- They trust on-the-water YouTube tests, dealers who publish their own tests (Echolotzentrum,
  Fishing World, Great Canadian Fishing Store, Ezoko), long-time forum members, owner groups.
- They compare Garmin LiveScope (Plus, XR), Lowrance ActiveTarget and Eagle Eye, Humminbird MEGA
  Live, Garmin Panoptix.

## 5. Their words (use these)

"live sonar", "forward-facing" / "FFS" / "FF", "scope", "live imaging", "black box", "graph"
(the fish finder), "flasher", "wheelhouse" (heated ice shack), "run and gun", "sharp shooting",
"greenbacks", "watch them come in", "see how they react", "no black box, two wires",
"keep your graph, add live", "cheaper than LiveScope", "app based".

Avoid "cheat" and "edge" with musky and tournament bass anglers; avoid "sharpshoot muskies"
in Ontario.

## 6. What would win over a skeptical angler

1. Independent, unedited side-by-side tests against LiveScope on the same fish, at the depths
   each group fishes.
2. The myths corrected on the first screen: own router, no cell service, not Bluetooth.
3. A cold-weather guide with measured battery drain and tested temperatures.
4. A whole-system cost comparison that counts the screen, plus kits (battery, case, pole or
   mount, optional tablet) like the ones German and Polish dealers already sell.
5. A visible app track record and a plain privacy statement.
6. A clear account of who Accuphy is and where support, warranty and engineering happen.
7. Ways to try it: demo days, ice-show demos, loaners, easy returns.
8. Owner stories from their own region and style, saying how long they have owned it.
9. A promise of continued firmware updates.

## 7. Survey questions to run

**Before purchase (Lab sign-up, show visitors, abandoned carts):** where and what they fish;
current electronics and whether to keep them; main use; what they compare against and its
price; budget for the whole system; which phone or tablet (model, size), would they buy one;
rank of worries (price, image, cold, battery, Wi-Fi, app, mounting, learning curve, brand trust,
warranty, resale, tournament rules, ethics); "does it need cell service or Bluetooth?" (myth
check); who would convince them; does "Canadian company" or country of manufacture matter;
what would make them buy now (trial, kit, returns, price).

**After purchase (one-question survey at checkout + a longer one at day 30):** how they heard of
it and why they chose it; what almost stopped them; setup time and mount; which screen device,
readable in sun?; dropouts or lag; battery drain per hour and coldest use; image vs expectation
per view; app confusion or crashes; first "wow" and first frustration; catch change; support
experience; would they recommend it; accessories wanted.
